Conditions driven by hormonal dysregulation, ovarian function, and uterine health

Hormonal · F2 F1 F6 F9 F4 F8
Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)
Women of reproductive age · 15–45 Strong app signal
Signal potential: 82%
▾
Population — India · Uganda · East Africa
India prevalence19.6% by Rotterdam criteria (≈44M women) ICMR Task Force, JAMA Netw Open 2024
Age of onsetTypically 15–25; peak diagnosis 20–35
Uganda / East AfricaEstimated 8–13% (WHO global estimate); limited local studies
Undiagnosed fractionUp to 70% undiagnosed globally; higher in LMICs Bozdag et al., Hum Reprod 2016
User groupAdolescent girls + women of reproductive age (12–45)
Emotional labelling — recommended spec (starting from zero)
Minimum fields needed from F1
Mood valence score — 1–5 daily. PCOS drives cyclical mood swings; pattern only detectable with daily timestamped scoring
Irritability flag — yes/no daily. Androgen-driven irritability is distinct from sadness and needs separate capture
Energy level — 1–5 daily. Insulin resistance in PCOS causes characteristic post-meal energy crashes
Timestamp — time of day. Cortisol rhythm disruption in PCOS shows morning-specific low energy pattern
Marginal gain: F2 (cycle dates) alone creates a PCOS signal candidate. F1 (to spec) + F2 + F6 (skin) together raise confidence to ~82% — covering the three most common presenting features: irregular cycles, mood dysregulation, and androgen-driven acne.
Data fields → signal strength
CoreF2
Menstrual cycle dates + regularity
Irregular cycles (>35 days or <3 periods/year) are the primary PCOS diagnostic criterion. Without this, the signal cannot be established.
SupportF6
Skin condition rating (acne)
Androgen-driven acne present in 70–80% of PCOS cases. Weekly skin rating creates a secondary confirming signal.
SupportF9
Body weight trend
43.2% of Indian women with PCOS have obesity (JAMA 2024). Rising weight + irregular cycles strengthens signal significantly.
EnrichF1
Emotional labelling (valence + irritability + energy)
Mood dysregulation present in most PCOS cases. Cyclical pattern co-moving with cycle irregularity strengthens signal.
EnrichF4
Fatigue — post-meal pattern
Post-meal fatigue from insulin resistance is characteristic of PCOS. Time-of-day sub-field is key.
WalletHW
Hormone panel (LH, FSH, testosterone, AMH) or pelvic ultrasound
Clinical confirmation. Converts probable signal to confirmed PCOS for pharma-grade use.

Women of reproductive age · 15–49 Requires pain + cycle
Signal potential: 75%
▾
Population — India · Uganda · East Africa
India~42 million women affected ICMR-NIRRCH, Am J Reprod Immunol 2022

Diagnostic delay7–10 year average gap; highest in LMICs

Emotional labelling — recommended spec (starting from zero)
Minimum fields needed from F1
Mood valence — 1–5 daily. Depression and anxiety co-occur in endometriosis at 3–4× background rate
Context tag — physical pain / hormonal / situational / none. Ties emotional state to physical pain episodes
Cycle-phase timestamp — enables cyclical mood pattern detection correlated with menstrual phase
Marginal gain: F10 (pain score) + F2 (cycle dates) create the core signal. Adding F7 (GI symptoms) moves confidence from ~50% to ~75%, covering dyschezia which affects 35% of cases.
Data fields → signal strength
CoreF10
Pain score NRS 0–10 + location + cycle correlation
Cyclical pelvic pain is the defining symptom. Daily NRS correlated with cycle phase is the primary signal field.
CoreF2
Menstrual cycle dates + pain score at menstruation
Dysmenorrhoea severity scored separately from chronic pelvic pain. Cyclical pattern distinguishes endometriosis from IBS or fibromyalgia.
SupportF7
GI symptoms (dyschezia, bloating)
Bowel involvement present in 35% of cases. GI symptoms co-moving with cycle phase is a distinctive pattern.
SupportF4
Fatigue severity
Systemic fatigue reported in >50% of endometriosis patients; co-moves with pain flares.
EnrichF1
Emotional labelling (mood + context tag)
Captures psychological burden and distinguishes flare-related mood change from baseline depression.
WalletHW
Surgical report or imaging (TVUS / MRI)

Hormonal · F1 F2 F3 F13
PMDD — Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder
Women of reproductive age · 18–45 Uniquely detectable by app
Signal potential: 88%
▾
Population — India · Uganda · East Africa
Global prevalence3–8% of menstruating women meet DSM-5 PMDD criteria ACOG; Epperson et al., JAMA 2012
India5–15% moderate-to-severe PMS/PMDD; under-diagnosed as "normal mood swings" Nisar et al., J Pak Med Assoc 2008
Age of peakLate 20s to early 40s; often worsens with age
User groupWomen of reproductive age (18–45) with regular cycles
Emotional labelling — recommended spec (starting from zero)
PMDD requires the richest emotional labelling spec of any condition
Mood valence — 1–5 daily with timestamp. PMDD pattern: severe low mood 7–10 days pre-menstruation, resolving within 48h of period onset
Irritability flag — yes/no daily. DSM-5 criterion — marked irritability distinct from sadness
Anxiety flag — yes/no daily. Tension and anxiety are core PMDD features distinct from general depression
Functional impact — did symptoms interfere with work/relationships? Severity anchor required for PMDD vs PMS distinction
Marginal gain: This is the condition where emotional labelling (built to spec) + cycle dates create a signal that clinical assessment alone cannot replicate — because it requires longitudinal daily data across two consecutive cycles. Signal potential 88% with F1 + F2 alone.
Data fields → signal strength
CoreF1
Emotional labelling — valence + irritability + anxiety + functional impact, daily timestamped
PMDD is defined by the cyclical timing of mood symptoms. Daily timestamped data correlated with cycle phase is the only way to detect it.
CoreF2
Menstrual cycle dates
Without cycle dates, the cyclical emotional pattern cannot be phase-aligned. Together these two fields are the complete PMDD signal.
SupportF3
Sleep quality
Insomnia is a PMDD criterion. Sleep disruption co-moving with luteal phase strengthens the signal.
EnrichF13
Stress context
Distinguishes stress-driven mood change from hormonally-driven PMDD pattern.

Iron-Deficiency Anaemia
All women — highest in pregnant + adolescents No clinical test needed for signal
Signal potential: 70%
▾
Population — India · Uganda · East Africa
India (NFHS-5 2019–21)57% of women of reproductive age anaemic; 52.2% of pregnant women BMC Public Health 2024; NFHS-5
Uganda24% women of childbearing age; 65.8% of pregnant women in sub-Saharan Africa PMC 2016; Gulu/Hoima hospital study
Age of highest burdenAdolescent girls (rapid growth demand) + pregnant women (increased iron need)
User groupAdolescent girls + women of reproductive age + pregnant women
Marginal gain: Fatigue (F4) + heavy cycle (F2) + nutrition adequacy (F5) creates the anaemia signal without any blood test. In populations where lab access is limited, this combination identifies probable anaemia — something no existing dataset provides for India and Uganda at scale.
Data fields → signal strength
CoreF4
Fatigue — exertional, present on waking
Fatigue on mild exertion is the cardinal symptom. Key sub-field: does fatigue worsen on climbing stairs or light activity?
SupportF2
Menstrual flow volume (heavy bleeding)

SupportF5
Nutrition — iron-rich food frequency
Low dietary iron is a primary cause, especially in vegetarian populations.
EnrichF8
Physical activity decline
Reduced exercise tolerance (distinct from disinterest) is an anaemia signal. Co-movement with fatigue strengthens it.
WalletHW
CBC — haemoglobin level
Hb <12g/dL confirms anaemia. Converts probable to confirmed signal for pharma-grade use.

2 conditions
Mental health · F1 F3 F8 F13 F4
Depression
All women · 15–65 Emotional labelling is the anchor
Signal potential: 78%
▾
Population — India · Uganda · East Africa
Uganda (meta-analysis)30.2% pooled prevalence; postpartum/pregnant women 26.9% PLOS ONE 2022; systematic review
India~15–20% of women; postnatal period 11–26% PMC 2015; rural Karnataka study
Age of peak15–45 (reproductive years); postpartum highest acute risk
User groupAll women; postpartum women at highest acute risk
Emotional labelling — recommended spec (starting from zero)
Minimum fields for a PHQ-9 proxy signal
Mood valence — 1–5 daily. Persistent low mood ≥2 weeks is the core depressive criterion
Interest / pleasure loss flag — "Did you enjoy activities today?" yes/no. Anhedonia is the second core criterion — separate from low mood
Energy rating — 1–5 daily. Persistent fatigue is PHQ-9 item 4
Functional impact — "Did symptoms make it hard to work or be with others today?" Severity anchor for PHQ-9 validation
Monthly embedded PHQ-2 — two validated questions; insert into monthly check-in as a screener
Marginal gain: F1 (to spec) + F3 (sleep) together create a depression signal stronger than many clinical tools — because it is longitudinal. Two weeks of daily mood + sleep produces a time-series that a single consultation cannot match.
Data fields → signal strength
CoreF1
Emotional labelling — valence + anhedonia + energy + functional impact, daily timestamped
Depression requires persistent symptoms across ≥2 weeks. Only longitudinal daily data captures this. Spec must include anhedonia separately from mood valence.
SupportF3
Sleep quality — duration + quality + early morning waking
Insomnia (especially early morning waking) is a core depressive feature. Sleep + mood co-movement is the strongest two-field depression signal.
SupportF8
Physical activity decline
Psychomotor retardation and social withdrawal are depression features. Activity decline correlated with mood is a behavioural marker.
EnrichF13
Stress and life events log
Contextualises mood data — distinguishes situational from clinical major depressive disorder.

Postnatal Depression (PND)
Postpartum women · 0–12 months post-birth High LMIC data gap
Signal potential: 85%
▾
Population — India · Uganda · East Africa
Uganda19.2–21.1% in community; up to 43% in some studies PMC 2020; Arach et al., PLOS ONE
India11–26.3%; most cases undetected by the health system PMC 2015; Karnataka postnatal study
Africa pooled17.8% pooled prevalence PLOS ONE 2020; Gelaye et al. meta-analysis
Screening gap>50% of cases undetected by healthcare providers — even in India
User groupPostpartum women (weeks 1–52 post-birth)
Emotional labelling — recommended spec for PND
Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) proxy — embed in monthly postpartum check-in
Mood over past week — 4-point scale. EPDS item 1 — sustained mood state, not daily fluctuation
Enjoyment of things — 4-point scale. EPDS item 2 — anhedonia in postpartum context
Self-blame out of proportion — EPDS item 3 — guilt is a specific PND feature often missed
Anxious or worried without good reason — EPDS items 4/5 — perinatal anxiety overlaps but is distinct
Feeling overwhelmed — EPDS item 10 — closest to suicidal ideation screening in the scale
Marginal gain: F14 (postpartum status) + F1 (EPDS-proxied emotional labelling) creates a signal that does not exist in any current structured dataset at scale from India and Uganda. Pharma buyers active in this space: Sage Therapeutics, J&J, multiple global health funders.
Data fields → signal strength
CoreF1
Emotional labelling — EPDS-proxied (mood, anhedonia, anxiety, guilt, overwhelm)
EPDS is the gold-standard PND screening tool. Embedding its items in a monthly postpartum check-in creates a validated proxy signal.
CoreF14
Postpartum status + weeks since delivery
PND is context-specific. The same emotional scores have different clinical meaning in postpartum period vs general population.
SupportF3
Sleep quality
Sleep deprivation + mood distinguishes PND from normal new-parent fatigue. Pattern co-movement is key.
EnrichF13
Social support context
"Do you feel supported at home?" — partner support absence is the strongest single PND predictor.

Thyroid disorders

Massively under-diagnosed in women — the multi-stream signal where cross-domain data has the greatest advantage

1 condition
Thyroid · F4 F9 F6 F1 F2 F3
Hypothyroidism
All women · 30–65; highest 45+ Multi-stream advantage
Signal potential: 72%
▾
Population — India · Uganda · East Africa
India10–12% of women; subclinical hypothyroidism 9.4%; women 10× more likely than men Unnikrishnan et al., Indian J Endocrinol Metab 2011
Postmenopausal women India27.3% prevalence — 1 in 3 menopausal women hypothyroid J Family Med Prim Care 2024; northern India study
East AfricaEstimated 5–10%; iodine deficiency historically high WHO Micronutrient Database
Diagnosis gap"Iceberg phenomenon" — symptoms vague, most diagnosed only when severe
User groupAll women 30+; postmenopausal women highest burden
Marginal gain: Hypothyroidism is where combining multiple low-friction streams creates a signal no single stream can approach alone. Fatigue + weight gain + dry skin + low mood + heavy periods together achieve 72% confidence — without any blood test. This is the multi-stream cross-domain advantage in its clearest form.
Data fields → signal strength
CoreF4
Fatigue — persistent, present on waking (not exertional)
Fatigue present in >90% of hypothyroid cases. Key distinction from anaemia: not worsened by specific activity, present on waking.
SupportF9
Unexplained weight gain
Weight gain despite unchanged diet/activity is characteristic. Trend over 3+ months is the signal, not a single reading.
SupportF6
Skin dryness / hair changes
Dry skin and hair loss are classic hypothyroid signs. Skin rating sub-field: "skin feels dry/rough" distinct from acne.
SupportF2
Heavy menstrual bleeding
Hypothyroidism causes heavy periods. Combined with other streams, this is a strong confirming signal.
EnrichF1
Emotional labelling — low mood + cognitive fog
Depression and cognitive slowing are hypothyroid features that co-occur with the physical signs above.
WalletHW
TSH blood test result
TSH >4.0 mIU/L = hypothyroidism. Definitive confirmation. Most commonly available blood test in India.

All women · 18–45 highest Gut-brain axis signal

SupportF1
Emotional labelling — stress and anxiety co-movement
Gut-brain axis: stress triggers GI symptoms in IBS. GI flare + high stress on same day is the defining cross-domain pattern.
SupportF13
Stress level and life events
Stress co-moving with GI symptoms across ≥3 episodes establishes the IBS pattern.
EnrichF5
Diet and food triggers log
Certain foods trigger IBS episodes. Diet log enables food-symptom correlation analysis.

Appendix — conditions for later consideration

These conditions are relevant but are either outside the primary population scope for Halla Health's current stage, require clinical infrastructure beyond app-only data collection, or have insufficient LMIC prevalence data to justify prioritising now. Listed for the record so the team can consciously park them.
Rheumatoid Arthritis
M05 / M06
Affects women 2–3× more than men. App signal (joint pain + fatigue + morning stiffness) is non-specific without blood markers.
Needs: F10 (morning stiffness sub-field) + HW (RF / anti-CCP blood test)
Lupus (SLE)
M32
90% female. App signal too non-specific (fatigue, joint pain, rash). Requires blood markers to distinguish from other conditions.
Needs: HW (ANA test) + F6 (butterfly rash flag) + F10 + F1
Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (ME/CFS)
G93.3
Women 2–4× more likely. App can contribute longitudinal fatigue data but diagnosis requires exclusion of other conditions first.
Needs: F4 (post-exertional malaise sub-field) + exclusion of F11, HW thyroid and anaemia
Coeliac Disease
K90.0
Signal via GI symptoms + nutrition, but prevalence in South Asia and East Africa limited. Requires antibody test for meaningful signal.
Needs: F7 + F5 (gluten exposure log) + HW (anti-tTG test)
GERD / Acid reflux
K21
High prevalence in India (~18%), detectable via F7 heartburn flag. Lower pharma DaaS value relative to other conditions at this stage.
Needs: F7 (heartburn sub-field) + F5 (dietary trigger log)
Premature Ovarian Insufficiency
E28.3
Rare (<2% under 40). App signal is cycle cessation + menopause symptoms in young women but population too small for current Halla Health scale.
Needs: F2 (cycle cessation) + F3 + F1 + HW (FSH level)
