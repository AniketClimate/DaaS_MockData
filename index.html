Signal architecture — what Halla Health can and cannot do
Critical framing: HPV infection is almost entirely asymptomatic. Halla Health data cannot detect HPV infection directly. What it can do is: (1) flag users who fit the epidemiological risk profile for HPV/cervical cancer and prompt them toward screening, (2) track vaccination status via F15 (medication record) and health wallet, and (3) capture early symptoms of advanced disease progression (abnormal discharge, intermenstrual bleeding, post-coital bleeding) — symptoms that indicate a woman may already have significant disease and needs urgent referral. This is a screening referral signal and a public health awareness tool, not an infection detection signal.
False positive risk — low for referral framing, high if framed as diagnosis. Risk factor profiling (age, reproductive history, number of pregnancies) will correctly flag many women who do not have HPV — and that is acceptable, because the output is a referral prompt for screening, not a diagnosis. The false positive cost is a screening appointment, not a clinical label. The false positive risk becomes high and harmful only if the signal is framed as "you likely have HPV" — which it must never be. The 52% sensitivity estimate reflects the proportion of high-risk women who would be correctly flagged for screening using the available fields.
Marginal gain: F15 (medication/vaccination record) capturing HPV vaccination status is the single highest-value addition — it enables population-level vaccine coverage mapping that does not exist at scale in India or Uganda. F2 (menstrual cycle) + F14 (pregnancy history) creates the reproductive risk profile. F10 (abnormal bleeding flag) is the clinical safety signal for advanced disease.

India's dyslipidaemia pattern — why it matters for signal design
The Indian lipid pattern is not the Western pattern. In Western populations, elevated LDL is the dominant risk. In India, low HDL-C (72.3% prevalence) and high triglycerides (29.5%) are the primary abnormalities — both driven by physical inactivity, abdominal obesity, and high refined-carbohydrate diet. This means the Halla Health signal model should weight lifestyle fields (F8 physical activity, F5 nutrition, F9 body weight/waist) more heavily than it would for a Western population. A clinical blood lipid test (health wallet) remains the confirmatory anchor — but the lifestyle composite creates a strong prior probability of low HDL / high triglycerides before any blood test is done.
False positive risk — moderate. The lifestyle risk factors for dyslipidaemia (obesity, inactivity, high-carb diet, stress) overlap substantially with those for hypertension, diabetes, and hypothyroidism. A user flagged for dyslipidaemia risk may also flag for 2–3 other metabolic conditions simultaneously. This is clinically appropriate — they are co-morbidities — but means the signal should be framed as "cardiovascular risk profile" rather than "cholesterol problem" at the user level. Specificity is higher in younger women (<40) where the other metabolic conditions are less prevalent.
Marginal gain: F9 (body weight trend) + F8 (physical activity) + F5 (nutrition — carbohydrate quality) creates a lifestyle dyslipidaemia risk composite with ~58% sensitivity for low HDL/high triglycerides in Indian women. Adding F12 (blood pressure) and F13 (stress) creates a full cardiovascular risk score. Health wallet lipid panel upload is the confirmatory anchor.

What the product team has agreed to build
The pilot builds two core capabilities: (1) completing the ASHA-woman data loop so ANC results (haemoglobin, blood pressure, blood group) can be securely shared with consent and an auditable trail; (2) merging the health wallet into the live consumer app. Data fields in scope include: ANC cards, delivery summaries, birth records, postnatal records, prescription records, diagnostic reports, immunisation cards, child growth records, and emotional wellbeing check-ins. Emotional wellbeing check-in — daily / weekly self-report
App-generated · Self-report in Marathi and Hindi · Longitudinal
App-generated ▾
Current state — partial implementation, not yet a clinical signal
Halla Health already captures basic emotional wellbeing (how a user feels today). However, the current implementation is not complete emotional labelling. To generate a clinically meaningful signal towards postnatal depression or antenatal depression, the check-in needs to be extended using a validated instrument — specifically a PHQ-9 proxy (for depression) or the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) (for PND). Without these structured components — mood valence, anhedonia, energy, functional impact, and a monthly embedded screener — the current check-in generates engagement data, not clinical signal. This is a planned development, not a current capability.
Disease signals generated — conditional on PHQ-9 / EPDS extension
Postnatal depression (PND) PLANNED
With EPDS extension: sustained low valence + low energy + functional impairment for 2+ weeks postpartum, combined with F14 (postpartum status), generates a PND proxy signal. The current basic check-in alone is insufficient — the EPDS 10-item monthly postpartum screen is required.
STRONG (with EPDS) · current state: weak
Antenatal depression PLANNED
With PHQ-9 proxy extension: depression during pregnancy (affecting 15–20% of Indian women) requires a monthly embedded PHQ-2 screener capturing sustained low mood and anhedonia during pregnancy. Current check-in does not capture anhedonia separately from mood.
MODERATE (with PHQ-9) · current state: weak
Stress & climate-related anxiety EXPLORATORY
Emotional wellbeing data co-analysed with climate event data (heatwaves, air quality alerts) can detect climate stress responses — relevant to the CARE-MNH climate-health framing. Novel signal with limited prior LMIC evidence; exploratory at this stage.
WEAK · exploratory, limited evidence

ABDM integration — what it unlocks for signal quality
ABDM ABHA ID linking converts individually uploaded records into an interoperable longitudinal health record linkable with public facility data. As of June 2023, 400 million Indians have ABHA IDs and 273 million have linked health records (Mishra et al., Health Systems & Reform, 2024 — doi:10.1080/23288604.2024.2392290). ABDM linkage means each wallet record can be correlated with facility records the woman does not manually upload — strengthening every signal above by adding provider-side data to the self-reported and ASHA-facilitated records already in the wallet.
Signal quality note — honest assessment
Signal strength depends entirely on upload rate and data completeness. Until field data is available from the pilot, signals rated "STRONG" should be treated as conditional on sustained user engagement and consistent record uploads. The embedded learning plan — user interviews, frontline worker focus groups, and structured monitoring — is designed to calibrate these estimates against real-world completeness before any external evidence claim is made.
